# HUB Offer and Revenue Architecture

> [!warning] Blueprint boundary
> This is a connected operating and commercial concept, not a validated offer catalogue, approved financial model or launch decision. Buyer, pricing, legal, delivery and recognition assumptions remain subject to refinement against [[00-project-control/gap-register/HUB_Project_Gap_Register|the gap register]].

## 1. Relationship between the HUB brand, HUB Negócios, Instituto HUB and Plataforma HUB

The **HUB brand and strategy** is the coordinating layer: it owns the narrative, C.A.O.S. method, standards, portfolio logic and group-level direction. It should make the system legible without making every activity appear to be the same product or legal service. The brand layer is therefore not itself a revenue bucket; it governs how offers are named, evidenced and connected.

**HUB Negócios** is the proposed commercial delivery unit. It would contract for business services, implementation, advisory work, ecosystem programs and commercial relationships, subject to confirmation of legal separation, authority and intercompany treatment (**STR-001**). Its commercial responsibility is to sell and deliver outcomes without representing hypotheses as guarantees.

**Instituto HUB** is the proposed mission-oriented unit for restricted impact, education and possibly grant- or sponsor-funded activity. Restricted funds, eligible costs, reporting and transfer boundaries must remain distinct from commercial receipts; the unit must not be used to blur commercial and restricted economics (**STR-001**, **FIN-002**). Its role is not automatically to subsidize commercial offers.

**Plataforma HUB** is the software, data, workflow and ecosystem infrastructure layer. It may be operated by a dedicated entity or through an arrangement not yet decided (**STR-001**). Platform capabilities are shared primitives—identity, intelligence, journey, solutions, connections, academy and recognition workflows—configured for offers but not casually re-sold as unrelated features. Subscription, licensing or usage revenue would require a defined owner and reporting treatment (**FIN-002**).

The intended relationship is a controlled loop: the brand defines the promise and method; HUB Negócios translates that method into paid work; Plataforma HUB captures repeatable workflows, evidence and network effects; Instituto HUB supports restricted-impact missions where funding and purpose permit. A project may involve all four, but contracts, data roles, IP, liability, delivery accountability and revenue ownership must be explicit rather than inferred (**STR-001**, **FIN-002**). The Selo HUB remains a potentially connected recognition capability, not a commercial guarantee: evaluation and implementation must be independent and conflict-controlled.

## 2. Three business fronts: offers, customers, partners and delivery responsibilities

The fronts are portfolio lenses, not separate silos. Each offer should identify a buying organization, participant beneficiaries, accountable unit, partner roles, platform configuration and evidence of value. Portfolio segmentation, budget ownership and buying process remain open (**GTM-001**).

| Business front | Blueprint offers | Likely customer / beneficiary types | Potential partners | Delivery responsibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mídia e Experiências** | Campaign or content strategy; inclusive events and experiences; employer/brand activation; insight-led communications; measurement and learning package. | Companies, institutions, associations, brands, event owners and ecosystem conveners; participants and audiences benefit. | Media, creative, event, cultural, research and distribution partners. | HUB Negócios owns scope, client relationship and commercial delivery; partners execute specialist components; Plataforma HUB supports audience, journey and evidence workflows; Instituto HUB participates only where purpose and restricted funding permit. |
| **Impacto Financiável** | Impact diagnosis; program architecture; funded intervention portfolio; monitoring, evaluation and learning; capability academy; restricted-impact reporting. | Institutions, foundations, public-interest funders, companies with impact budgets, associations and program participants. | Funders, evaluators, NGOs, educational bodies, public institutions and implementation organizations. | Instituto HUB is the conceptual home for restricted-purpose activity; HUB Negócios may provide separately contracted commercial services; Plataforma HUB provides controlled measurement and workflow infrastructure; independent evaluators protect evidence and recognition integrity. |
| **Ecossistemas Empresariais** | Ecosystem intelligence; supplier and talent discovery; qualified connections and opportunity matching; procurement and capability programs; enterprise platform access; implementation and evolution. | Institutions, federations, associations, enterprise buyers, procurement/RH leaders, suppliers, specialists and opportunity owners. | Associations, federations, HR/procurement/CRM providers, suppliers, specialists, financial and institutional networks. | HUB Negócios owns commercial program and implementation accountability; Plataforma HUB operates shared identity, intelligence, journey, connections and solutions capabilities; partners contribute supply and access but do not control HUB evidence or customer commitments. |

The C.A.O.S. delivery spine is common: **Contexto** diagnoses the buyer and ecosystem; **Arquitetura** defines the target state, offer scope and measures; **Operação** executes content, connections, interventions or journeys; **Sustentação** measures, governs, learns and evolves. This preserves one vocabulary while allowing a media project, a funded program and an enterprise ecosystem contract to have different outputs and commercial terms.

Offer boundaries should be recorded in an offer-to-buyer-to-capability matrix: who signs, who pays, who participates, what is delivered, which platform modules are used, what partner is needed, what evidence can be produced and which unit bears risk. That matrix is the required refinement for **STR-002** and must be reconciled with the portfolio segmentation work in [[04-project-management/tasks/BP-007_HUB_Go_To_Market_and_Partnerships]] (dependency on BP-007; do not infer its final segments here).

## 3. Primary, secondary and expansion revenue engines

The hierarchy below is sequencing logic, not a forecast or approval. The primary engine should fund useful delivery and generate repeatable evidence; secondary engines should increase retention and customer value; expansion engines should follow validated capability, governance and distribution rather than precede them (**FIN-002**, **STR-003**).

### Primary engine: implementation-led commercial adoption

The initial commercial spine is proposed as paid diagnosis, architecture and implementation for institutions, companies and ecosystem owners, with a bounded transition into recurring platform/service access. This reflects the current assumption that human-led operations reveal what can later be productized. A primary engagement could combine a diagnostic, a C.A.O.S. roadmap, configured workflow, partner/solution curation and follow-through. It is not a promise of a particular ROI.

Primary contracts could be originated through HUB Negócios and use Plataforma HUB as enabling infrastructure. Scope, acceptance, change control, support, data processing, IP and liability must be explicit. Conversion from implementation to recurring access is a hypothesis to test, not an assumed renewal (**STR-002**, **GTM-001**).

### Secondary engines: recurring access and repeat programs

Secondary revenue may include enterprise or ecosystem subscriptions/licenses, recurring intelligence and measurement retainers, managed journey operations, academy/capability programs, and repeat media/experience or impact-program contracts. Recurrence requires a defined service boundary, renewal trigger, denominator, support cost and owner. Subscription revenue must not include unearned implementation obligations, restricted funds or marketplace pass-throughs (**FIN-002**).

### Expansion engines: network and portfolio leverage

Potential expansion engines include marketplace fees for qualified solutions or transactions; sponsored or partner-funded ecosystem programs; benchmark or intelligence products subject to consent and anonymization; media and experience production at scale; and recognition or Selo-related services only under independent governance. These engines depend on product boundaries, data rights, measurement lineage, partner concentration controls and Selo independence (**STR-003**, **FIN-002**). They should be gated by evidence and operating capacity, not treated as current traction.

The full commercial logic is therefore: paid implementation creates configured value and learning; recurring access sustains workflows and measurement; marketplace, network, media, funded-program and recognition-adjacent opportunities expand the system when their separate controls are satisfied. The roadmap must connect these motions to M0–M4 product, data, governance and launch exit criteria rather than imply simultaneous availability (**STR-003**).

## 4. Revenue classifications and ownership boundaries

The following taxonomy prevents category collisions. Classification is conceptual until finance defines recognition, timing, contract allocation, tax treatment, source-of-truth ledger and reporting rules (**FIN-002**).

| Classification | Economic meaning | Illustrative HUB activity | Primary owner / boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial revenue** | Consideration from a customer for a business-purpose offer. | Advisory, enterprise access, managed program, media/experience service. | Contracting commercial unit (proposed HUB Negócios); not Institute restricted funding. |
| **Implementation revenue** | Paid setup, configuration, migration, onboarding, diagnosis or change work required to activate an offer. | C.A.O.S. diagnosis, tenant configuration, workflow design, data onboarding, training. | Delivery owner and contracting unit; scope and acceptance must be separated from recurring service obligations. |
| **Recurring revenue** | Contracted periodic access or service with an ongoing obligation. | Platform subscription/license, intelligence retainer, managed journey, support or academy access. | Platform and/or commercial service owner as contractually defined; requires renewal and service metrics. |
| **Marketplace revenue** | Fees or commissions associated with a qualified connection, supplier service or transaction. | Match fee, supplier commission, referral or transaction fee. | Marketplace operator with disclosure, conflict, tax, liability and data controls; never count gross pass-through as HUB revenue without a rule. |
| **Project revenue** | Fixed- or milestone-based consideration for a bounded campaign, event, funded program or bespoke intervention. | Media project, event, impact program, research or ecosystem activation. | Contracting unit and named project owner; deliverables, milestones and partner pass-throughs must be traceable. |
| **Restricted-impact revenue/funding** | Funds legally or contractually restricted to an impact, education or mission purpose. | Institute grant, donation, sponsorship or restricted program funding. | Instituto HUB (conceptually), with restricted ledger, eligible-cost rules and reporting; cannot be silently reclassified to commercial revenue. |

One contract may contain multiple classifications, but allocation must be documented at obligation level. Implementation should not be presented as ARR; marketplace transaction volume should not be presented as HUB revenue; influenced customer value should not be presented as realized cash; restricted funding should not be counted toward commercial recurring revenue. These distinctions support the value-state principles in [[01-blueprint/strategy/HUB_Project_Blueprint_Foundation]] and require finance refinement (**FIN-002**).

## 5. Assumptions, unresolved decisions and dependencies across the blueprint

The architecture assumes that one connected HUB system can support distinct unit responsibilities, three fronts and staged monetization without category confusion. It also assumes that institutional distribution, verified implementation evidence and a shared semantic layer can compound value; none is validated in this Blueprint (**STR-001**, **STR-002**, **STR-003**, **GTM-001**). The following dependencies are explicit:

- **Depends on BP-002:** product/module boundaries, packaging, permissions and implementation-versus-platform behavior. This document must not invent the minimum product or supported surfaces.
- **Depends on BP-003:** data, intelligence, measurement and value lineage needed to define evidence, usage metrics and defensible customer value.
- **Depends on BP-004:** technology/integration sequencing and cost assumptions that constrain delivery economics and recurring support.
- **Depends on BP-005:** governance, legal, entity, IP, LGPD, liability and Selo independence boundaries, including whether the four-unit architecture is legally executable.
- **Depends on BP-006:** finance, recognition, scenarios, cost allocation, restricted-fund treatment and KPI definitions; no figures in this note are financially approved.
- **Depends on BP-007:** portfolio segments, buyer roles, budgets, buying processes, routes, partner obligations and fallback channels.
- **Depends on BP-008:** integrated roadmap, phase exit criteria and launch/readiness gates connecting the full system (**STR-003**).

## Cross-Blueprint Dependencies

This BP-001 artifact is the commercial spine for the other blueprint documents, but it cannot unilaterally settle their interfaces. Its next refinement should be a traceability matrix connecting every offer to [[01-blueprint/strategy/HUB_Project_Blueprint_Foundation]], the product capability model, C.A.O.S. stages, data/metric lineage, entity and contract boundaries, financial recognition scenarios, buyer evidence and integrated launch gates. Until those dependencies are refined and approved, all offer names, ownerships, revenue priorities and customer assumptions remain Blueprint concepts.

The dependency order is intentionally staged: resolve unit and offer relationships (**STR-001**, **STR-002**); define product and operating boundaries; establish data and value semantics; define legal, LGPD, IP, liability and Selo controls; translate architecture into technical and operational specifications; rebuild finance and GTM scenarios; then assemble approval evidence and the integrated launch gate (**STR-003**, **FIN-002**, **GTM-001**). Parallel drafting by BP-002–BP-008 is expected; unresolved interfaces must remain visible rather than being silently filled with invented content.

## 6. Blueprint traceability and maturity statement

This document connects the offer architecture to the project’s identity, C.A.O.S. method, six conceptual platform modules, three business fronts, data/value chain, governance foundation, M0–M4 sequencing and commercial hypotheses in [[01-blueprint/strategy/HUB_Project_Blueprint_Foundation]]. It is intended to prevent category collisions and make ownership questions explicit, while preserving room for refinement. It does not claim validated demand, confirmed partners, production capability, approved pricing, evidence-certified ROI, financial approval or launch readiness. Every unresolved decision remains linked to one or more registered gaps and must be revisited before promotion from Blueprint to Refinement or Approval.
