# HUB — Investor-readiness transformation plan

> **Objective:** Transform the current HUB master document from a broad ecosystem blueprint into a focused, evidence-backed investment thesis without modifying the original v1 file.

## 1. Purpose and transformation objective

The current document is strong as a conceptual architecture: it explains the HUB group, its portfolio, the C.A.O.S. method, the Plataforma HUB, the Selo HUB, partner ecosystems, and the intended roadmap.

The enhanced version must answer the questions an investor, board member, or strategic funder will ask immediately:

1. Which customer buys first?
2. What urgent problem does HUB solve for that customer?
3. What evidence proves the problem and the willingness to pay?
4. What is the first repeatable product and revenue engine?
5. Why can HUB win against existing alternatives?
6. What becomes difficult to copy over time?
7. What capital, team, and milestones are required?
8. What evidence would cause HUB to change or stop the strategy?

The enhanced version is not a longer description of the same ambition. It is a narrower argument supported by evidence, economics, explicit exclusions, and measurable decision gates.

### Pilot-first operating thesis

The immediate business is a founder-led, manually delivered institutional pilot. Founders perform and measure discovery, setup, diagnosis review, curation, matching, follow-through, and outcome reporting; software supports the work but does not justify premature platform build. The broad platform vision remains conditional future scope.

### Exact active scope

The first operating cycle contains exactly **one buyer type, one buyer, one opportunity, one pilot, one workflow, and one measurable outcome**. These must be locked before execution. No additional buyer, cohort, workflow variant, or metric is active before the expansion gate.

### Deferred / not active list

Explicitly deferred and not active during the pilot: **marketplace, Selo, Institute, media/events, Academy, broad platform, multiple cohorts, deep integrations, and autonomous recommendations**. These remain options or historical architecture, not validated demand or current commitments.

## 2. What v2 preserves and changes

### Preserve

- HUB’s central promise: **“Diferenças que movimentam negócios.”**
- The diagnosis → plan → connect → implement → measure → recognize journey.
- The C.A.O.S. method: Contexto → Arquitetura → Operação → Sustentação.
- The four-unit architecture, subject to legal and governance validation.
- The evidence-based integrity principle of the Selo HUB.
- The Plataforma HUB as the long-term infrastructure for ecosystems.
- Sebrae, Firjan, and other partner applications as strategic possibilities, clearly labeled by evidence status.
- The original v1 document as the historical and conceptual reference.

### Change

- Replace the broad initial audience with one beachhead buyer and one initial use case.
- Separate proven facts, hypotheses, decisions, options, and long-term aspirations.
- Move unvalidated portfolio lines out of the initial operating strategy.
- Add market evidence, customer evidence, competitive analysis, pricing, unit economics, capital requirements, and traction.
- Convert metrics into targets, review cadences, owners, and decision thresholds.
- Define what HUB will refuse during the first 12–18 months.
- Make the commercial model and the Selo HUB independence architecture explicit.

## 3. Investor-readiness definition

The v2 strategy is ready for investor or strategic-funder circulation only when all of the following are true:

- One initial buyer and use case are named.
- The buyer’s problem is supported by documented interviews or paid demand.
- At least one pilot has a written scope, price, owner, timeline, and success criteria.
- The primary revenue engine for the first 18 months is explicit.
- Pricing, cost-to-serve, sales cycle, gross margin, and renewal assumptions are modeled.
- Alternatives and competitors are mapped against the chosen beachhead.
- The moat hypothesis explains how data, distribution, trust, methodology, or workflow compounding will develop.
- The team and capability gaps are visible.
- Capital required, use of funds, milestones, and downside scenarios are documented.
- The Selo HUB has credible independence safeguards.
- Data, IP, legal-unit, and partner-rights assumptions are documented.
- Every major assumption has a validation method and a decision threshold.

## 4. Strategic narrowing

### 4.1 Select the beachhead buyer

Evaluate candidate buyers using the same criteria:

| Criterion | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Pain severity | The buyer describes a costly or urgent problem in its own words. |
| Budget ownership | A named role controls or influences the relevant budget. |
| Buying urgency | There is a deadline, compliance pressure, market opportunity, or strategic priority. |
| Distribution leverage | One sale can reach multiple participating organizations. |
| Adoption feasibility | The buyer can authorize data, participation, and pilot access. |
| Renewal potential | The problem recurs often enough to support an annual relationship. |
| Reference value | A successful pilot would improve credibility with similar buyers. |

The output must select one initial buyer rather than keeping entities, federations, associations, networks, and companies equally prioritized.

### 4.2 Select the initial use case

Choose one use case that can prove the complete HUB journey within the first pilot. The selected use case must define:

- Initial participant profile
- Triggering problem
- Diagnostic input
- Evidence collected
- Recommended intervention
- Connection or solution supplied
- Outcome measured
- Renewal or expansion event

Potential use cases may remain in an options appendix, but only one can be the initial strategy.

### 4.3 Define exclusions

For the first 12–18 months, explicitly exclude:

- Unrelated media and event expansion
- Multiple independent buyer segments
- Fully automated seal decisions
- Full national marketplace liquidity goals
- Deep integrations without a confirmed customer need
- Partner-specific features that do not improve the core product
- International expansion

Each exclusion must state what opportunity is being declined and why focus has higher expected value.

Research claims retain their historical meaning and must not be rewritten as validated customer, market, outcome, or traction evidence.

## 5. Evidence plan

### 5.1 Customer discovery

Run structured interviews with the selected buyer and participating organizations. Each interview must capture:

- Current workflow
- Current cost or effort
- Existing alternative
- Most painful failure point
- Decision-maker and budget owner
- Required proof of value
- Data and legal objections
- Price reaction to a defined pilot
- Conditions for renewal

Record interview evidence in a separate evidence log. Do not treat positive statements such as “interesting” or “we should talk” as traction.

### 5.2 Pilot validation

The first pilot must have:

- Written problem statement
- Named buyer and executive sponsor
- Defined participant cohort
- Paid or explicitly subsidized commercial terms
- Start and end dates
- Baseline measurement
- Product scope
- Implementation responsibilities
- Data permissions
- Success thresholds
- Renewal or expansion decision

### 5.3 Evidence classification

Every claim in v2 must be labeled as one of:

- **Proven:** supported by a signed agreement, measured result, or reliable source.
- **Observed:** supported by interviews or operational evidence but not yet statistically validated.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible and testable, but not yet confirmed.
- **Decision:** intentionally selected direction.
- **Option:** retained future possibility, not part of the current operating plan.

## 6. Market and competitive validation

### 6.1 Market sizing

Build a bottom-up market model for the selected beachhead:

- Number of reachable buyers
- Number of organizations per buyer
- Expected annual contract value
- Expected implementation revenue
- Adoption rate assumptions
- Renewal assumptions
- Serviceable geographic market
- Three-year reachable revenue opportunity

Use a conservative, base, and upside scenario. Avoid relying only on broad industry reports or global market-size estimates.

### 6.2 Alternative map

Compare HUB against the customer’s actual alternatives:

- Doing nothing
- Internal teams and spreadsheets
- Traditional consulting
- Certification or seal providers
- Association/member platforms
- ESG, HR, procurement, or learning software
- Marketplaces and directories
- Custom technology projects

For each alternative, document customer cost, strengths, weaknesses, switching friction, and the specific reason to choose HUB.

### 6.3 Defensibility thesis

The v2 document must select the primary compounding advantage. Candidates include:

- Longitudinal maturity and outcome data
- Institutional distribution
- Verified implementation evidence
- Trusted independent recognition
- Proprietary diagnostic methodology
- Curated supplier and talent performance data
- Embedded evolution workflows and switching costs

The plan must explain how the chosen advantage becomes stronger with every customer, pilot, or measured outcome.

## 7. Business model and financial model

### 7.1 Choose the primary economic engine

Select one primary engine for the first 18 months:

- Ecosystem licensing
- Enterprise subscription
- Implementation-led platform adoption
- Diagnostic and evolution program

Other revenue lines may remain as expansion paths, but they must not compete for equal priority during the initial period.

### 7.2 Define pricing and packaging

Create a commercial table covering:

- Discovery or diagnostic offer
- Pilot package
- Annual subscription or license
- Implementation fee
- Optional modules
- Renewal terms
- Seal evaluation and renewal terms
- Partner customization limits
- Discount authority

Every package must have a target customer, outcome, delivery cost, price range, and expansion path.

### 7.3 Build unit economics

Model at minimum:

- Average contract value
- Gross revenue and net revenue
- Implementation hours and cost
- Software cost per account
- Curatorial and support cost
- Customer acquisition cost
- Sales-cycle length
- Payback period
- Gross margin by revenue line
- Activation rate
- Renewal and expansion rate
- Cost to serve one additional organization

The model must reveal whether HUB is initially services-led, software-led, or a deliberate hybrid with a defined transition path.

### 7.4 Define capital and use of funds

Specify:

- Capital required for 12–18 months
- Hiring plan
- Product and technology budget
- Legal, data, and seal-governance budget
- Sales and pilot budget
- Operating reserve
- Milestones unlocked by each funding tranche
- Plan if external funding is delayed

## 8. Go-to-market and sales motion

Document the repeatable path from first contact to renewal:

1. Target account selection
2. Executive sponsor access
3. Problem discovery
4. Paid diagnostic or pilot proposal
5. Legal and data review
6. Pilot implementation
7. Outcome review
8. Annual renewal or ecosystem expansion

Define for each stage:

- Owner
- Expected duration
- Conversion threshold
- Required evidence
- Common objection
- Response or proof asset

The strategy must also define the first distribution channel: founder network, institutional partnership, direct enterprise sales, association channel, or another specific route.

## 9. Product and platform sequencing

### 9.1 Pilot product

Build only the smallest product that proves:

- Organization onboarding
- Configurable diagnosis
- Evidence submission
- Maturity result
- Evolution plan
- Curated recommendation
- Human-assisted connection
- Progress tracking
- Outcome report

### 9.2 Post-pilot product gates

Do not build additional modules unless pilot evidence proves the need. Gate each expansion by:

- Repeated customer demand
- Measurable usage
- Revenue contribution
- Reduced manual effort
- Reusable core functionality
- Acceptable data and support burden

### 9.3 White-label controls

Define three categories:

- **Standard:** available to every partner.
- **Configurable:** permitted within approved boundaries.
- **Prohibited:** creates a separate product, weakens data integrity, or undermines methodology.

## 10. Team, governance, and Selo HUB independence

### 10.1 Capability map

Map current versus required capability in:

- Institutional sales
- Product management
- Software delivery
- Data governance
- Methodology
- Implementation and customer success
- Marketplace curation
- Financial management
- Legal and tax structure
- Impact measurement

For each gap, define whether it will be filled by an existing leader, hire, contractor, partner, or technology provider.

### 10.2 Seal independence

Create a governance design that specifies:

- Appointment and removal of evaluators
- Evaluation payment flow
- Separation between implementation data and evaluation decisions
- Conflict-of-interest declarations
- Appeals and complaints process
- Evidence-access rules
- Suspension and withdrawal authority
- Public communication requirements
- Conditions under which a commercial client may or may not be evaluated

The seal must be credible even when a company rejects HUB implementation services.

### 10.3 Group and entity governance

Define the legal, financial, IP, data, and operational boundary between:

- HUB brand and strategy
- HUB Negócios
- Instituto HUB
- Plataforma HUB

## 11. Legal, data, and IP workstream

Document decisions for:

- Data-controller and processor roles
- Sensitive-data collection and consent
- Cross-ecosystem data permissions
- Aggregated and derived-data ownership
- Data retention and deletion
- Evidence retention for seal decisions
- Methodology ownership and licensing
- Partner-specific content and customizations
- Brand and “Powered by HUB” rights
- Liability for recommendations, matching, and recognition

## 12. Evidence stages and decision gates

### Evidence Stage 1 — Operating constraints

Define the exact active scope, founder-led manual workflow, roles, data/legal constraints, baseline/endline, and one measurable outcome.

Gate: the operating boundary is explicit enough to run one bounded pilot.

### Evidence Stage 2 — Buyer validation

Deliver:

- Beachhead buyer decision
- Initial use-case decision
- Interview evidence log
- Alternative map
- Pilot design
- Data and legal risk review

Gate: proceed only if the selected buyer confirms a painful problem, an accessible decision-maker, and a credible path to paid or formally funded pilot demand.

### Evidence Stage 3 — Paid/funded pilot

Deliver:

- Pilot contract or written funding agreement
- Baseline measurement
- Minimum product
- Implementation playbook
- Outcome report
- Renewal or expansion proposal

Gate: proceed only if the pilot produces measurable customer value, founder workload is logged, and delivery economics can be estimated.

### Evidence Stage 4 — Repeatability / expansion decision

Deliver:

- Standard package
- Pricing and implementation model
- Reusable product components
- Sales playbook
- Case study
- Renewal or repeat-purchase evidence from the buyer or a comparable buyer

Gate: authorize platform build or any additional business front only if paid/funded pilot evidence, founder workload, delivery economics, outcome evidence, and renewal/repeat-purchase evidence all exist. Otherwise remain manual and narrow scope.

This four-stage sequence replaces simultaneous-expansion roadmap language. Additional customers, cohorts, platform modules, Selo, Institute, marketplace, Academy, media/events, deep integrations, and autonomous recommendations are conditional future scope after the gate, not parallel commitments.

## 13. Kill criteria and scenario planning

The strategy must define explicit responses to negative evidence.

Before the first pilot begins, record numeric thresholds for the two currently qualitative controls below:

- **Marketplace liquidity threshold:** minimum qualified demands, completed matches, and repeat demand within a defined review period.
- **Partner concentration limit:** maximum percentage of revenue, active organizations, or pipeline that may depend on one partner.

The thresholds must be written into the pilot scorecard before results are known. They must not be changed after a negative result merely to avoid triggering the prescribed response.

| Signal | Required response |
|---|---|
| Buyers praise the concept but will not fund a pilot | Revisit the problem, buyer, or value proof before building more technology. |
| Diagnosis completion is low | Simplify the journey and reassess participant incentive. |
| Implementation requires excessive manual effort | Narrow scope or redesign the operating model before scaling. |
| No measurable outcome appears in the pilot | Stop expansion and revise the intervention hypothesis. |
| Renewal interest is weak | Do not assume the product is recurring; reassess ongoing value. |
| Marketplace demand remains below the defined liquidity threshold | Defer marketplace expansion and focus on a single demand-led workflow. |
| Partner customization repeatedly breaks standardization | Enforce configuration limits or reject the deal. |
| Seal independence cannot be credibly protected | Separate, redesign, or suspend the recognition product. |
| One partner exceeds the concentration limit | Diversify distribution before expanding the dependent channel. |

Create conservative, base, and upside scenarios for market adoption, sales cycle, cost-to-serve, and funding availability.
